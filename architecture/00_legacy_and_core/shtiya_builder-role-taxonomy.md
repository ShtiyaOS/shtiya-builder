# **Shtiya Builder Enterprise Architecture: Sub-Role Taxonomy and Workflow Matrix**

The enterprise architecture of the Shtiya Builder platform represents a paradigm shift in multi-sided PropTech marketplaces, synthesizing decentralized finance, spatial analytics, and strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC). Operating at the nexus of real estate development, legal compliance, and capital deployment, the system requires a rigid permission framework to prevent access drift, ensure regulatory compliance, and orchestrate complex multi-party workflows across disparate stakeholder groups1. By leveraging the native $SHQL token for value transfer and smart contracts for conditional escrow, the platform automates construction draw schedules, block assembly syndications, and municipal compliance tracking3.  
Historically, managing access for a handful of users in a linear application is straightforward; managing permissions for thousands of distinct entities across intersecting applications, decentralized financial ledgers, and municipal jurisdictions requires an advanced RBAC matrix5. The Shtiya Builder architecture relies on JSON-defined RBAC schemas coupled with JSON Web Tokens (JWT) to explicitly delineate view, edit, export, and administrative capabilities for each user persona6. Furthermore, the system integrates complex spatial data—such as Alquist-Priolo Earthquake Fault Zones, Standard Urban Storm Water Mitigation Plan (SUSMP) requirements, and local Senate Bill 9 (SB 9\) zoning constraints—directly into the Interactive Block Assembly Map, rendering personalized, role-specific data layers contingent on the user's programmatic key8.  
This comprehensive report details the granular sub-role taxonomy across the seven core stakeholder groups. The ensuing analysis delineates the exact permission boundaries, visual interfaces, cross-application triggers, and financial ledgers governing the twenty-two specialized sub-roles within the Shtiya Builder ecosystem, ensuring absolute separation of duties and auditable state transitions.

## **Group 1: The Owner Entity**

The Owner group represents the foundational supply side of the real estate marketplace. However, treating all property owners as a monolith introduces severe inefficiencies and user experience friction. The RBAC architecture segregates this group based on their fundamental intent, financial health, and spatial relationship to neighboring parcels, isolating distinct workflows to prevent speculative noise and ensure regulatory compliance.  
Operating under the owner\_single programmatic key, the Single Homeowner sub-role is engineered specifically for individuals seeking to extract utility or financial equity from their primary residence without engaging in complex municipal rezoning or speculative block assemblies. The primary motivation driving this persona is the modernization of their underlying asset and the optimization of their residential balance sheet. The key pain point eliminated by the Shtiya Builder platform is the historic friction associated with sourcing reliable, vetted contractors and navigating the opaque, paper-heavy home equity lending market. By isolating this user from high-density assembly solicitations, the system protects them from unwanted commercial pressure while focusing the interface entirely on property maintenance, localized improvement, and equity extraction.  
Within the Interactive Map View, the owner\_single is restricted to an egocentric spatial rendering. The map isolates their specific parcel boundary, overlaying real-time Automated Valuation Model (AVM) estimates, local comparable sales, and localized permit activities within a limited radius. When the user interacts with their own parcel marker, the system triggers a localized action menu, permitting the initiation of maintenance tickets, the request for renovation quotes from vetted tradesmen, or the submission of a home equity query. Clicking on neighboring parcels yields only anonymized, publicly available tax data, explicitly suppressing owner identities to enforce strict privacy standards and prevent localized data scraping.  
Cross-group integrations for this sub-role are strictly bounded. The owner\_single interacts primarily with the General Contractor for project scoping and the Retail Equity Lender for financing. When a renovation quote is approved within the platform, the system generates an automated data payload directed to the Retail Equity Lender, who immediately evaluates the post-renovation AVM to underwrite the required credit line.  
Financially, this sub-role interacts with the ecosystem through the $SHQL ledger and Web3 escrow mechanisms. Upon securing a home equity line, the lender funds a smart contract escrow specifically tied to the renovation scope4. The owner\_single utilizes a multi-signature interface to authorize partial draw releases in $SHQL to the General Contractor upon the completion of predefined work packages. This deterministic state transition ensures that the homeowner's capital is only deployed when physical progress is authenticated, protecting their newly extracted equity from contractor default or abandonment.  
The owner\_investor sub-role assumes a highly speculative and collaborative posture, focusing on the aggregation of adjacent parcels to unlock higher density zoning designations. This persona actively seeks to maximize the Floor Area Ratio (FAR) of their collective holdings through neighborhood assemblies, leveraging municipal initiatives like California's Senate Bill 9 (SB 9\) which allows for urban lot splits and two-unit developments in single-family zones8. The driving motivation is the realization of asymmetric financial upside through collective bargaining and land use optimization. The critical pain point resolved by the platform is the historic inability to securely organize fragmented neighborhood ownership, mathematically align financial interests, and collectively market a contiguous urban block to institutional developers without catastrophic holdout risk.  
The Interactive Map View for the owner\_investor features complex spatial aggregations and hypothetical massing models. The map renders zoning overlays, highlighting underutilized FAR, specific SB 9 lot split eligibility requirements such as minimum 1,200 square foot parcel sizes, and the opt-in status of adjacent properties8. Parcels are color-coded based on the neighbors' verified willingness to participate in a block committee. Clicking a parcel marker initiates complex legal actions, allowing the user to transmit digital Joint Venture (JV) intent agreements, propose neighborhood voting initiatives, or calculate the aggregated valuation of a multi-lot assembly. The system dynamically runs spatial queries to ensure the proposed assembly meets the minimum acreage thresholds required by local municipalities for high-density rezoning petitions.  
Service integrations for this persona are sophisticated, interfacing primarily with the Master Assembler, the Zoning Consultant, and the Joint Venture Equity Partner. The owner\_investor can programmatically authorize the Zoning Consultant to conduct a feasibility study on the assembled lots, the results of which are broadcasted directly to the Master Assembler marketplace to solicit bulk purchase offers.  
Financial triggers involve multi-party smart contract syndications acting as credible commitments14. When a block committee reaches a consensus, each owner\_investor signs an on-chain transaction locking their parcel's digital deed into a decentralized escrow. The Joint Venture Equity Partner or Master Assembler deposits $SHQL earnest money into this same contract. The funds are mathematically locked and subsequently dispersed based on the execution of a Master Agreement, ensuring that no single homeowner can breach the assembly intent without forfeiting their algorithmic deposit, thereby removing the primary friction of neighborhood syndication4.  
Assigned the owner\_distressed key, the Distressed Homeowner identifies properties burdened by severe municipal tax liens, pre-foreclosure proceedings, or catastrophic Department of Building (DOB) and local code enforcement violations. The primary motivation is rapid asset preservation, aggressive loss mitigation, and the avoidance of irreversible legal or financial judgments. The core friction removed by the architecture is the overwhelming complexity of municipal bureaucracy, code enforcement hearings, and predatory wholesale tactics. The system functions as a defensive legal shield, providing immediate, structured pipelines to specialized legal and financial remediation without exposing the user to speculative exploitation.  
In the Interactive Map View, the data layers undergo a drastic contextual shift to reflect the urgency of the user's state. The map highlights the user's property with specific municipal hazard indicators, such as active Stop Work Orders (SWO), severe code enforcement actions concerning structural maintenance or illegal garage conversions, and complex lien priority stacks16. Clicking the parcel marker bypasses traditional development tools entirely and immediately surfaces emergency workflow pipelines such as Halt Foreclosure, Lift Stop Work Order, or Cure Municipal Violation. The map explicitly suppresses unsolicited pings from Wholesalers unless the user toggles a specific Seeking Exit flag.  
Cross-group integrations automatically route the owner\_distressed to the specialized Legal Shield group. Initiating a violation cure immediately pings the Municipal Violation Expediter to schedule an Environmental Control Board (ECB) or local code enforcement hearing. Concurrently, if the property is flagged for mortgage default, the Loss Mitigation Specialist receives strictly scoped, read-only access to the user's financial telemetry to begin structuring a loan modification with the servicing bank.  
The financial interactions utilize $SHQL micro-escrows to retain critical legal representation. Because distressed owners typically lack immediate liquidity, the smart contracts allow external liquidity providers to post a curing bond in $SHQL directly to the Legal Expediter's escrow. In exchange, the capital provider receives a tokenized lien position or an exclusive purchase option on the property, ensuring the emergency legal work is funded while preserving the homeowner's immediate cash flow and providing a structured path to solvency15.

## **Group 2: The Investor / Developer**

This group provides the speculative energy, risk capital, and localized market expertise necessary to reposition distressed assets and orchestrate complex land assemblies. The RBAC model segments this group based on their specific risk tolerance, capital deployment scale, and preferred phase of the real estate life cycle, ensuring that users only access the data and workflows relevant to their operational model.  
Operating under the investor\_wholesaler programmatic key, the Wholesaler or Lead Scout functions as the decentralized acquisition arm of the platform. This user identifies off-market opportunities, secures equitable interest via purchase options, and rapidly assigns those contracts to capitalized developers. The driving motivation is the maximization of capital velocity through contract arbitrage. The wholesaler seeks to minimize holding periods and maximize contract assignment fees. The critical pain point addressed by the platform is the highly manual, error-prone nature of localized lead generation, title verification, and contract assignment disputes. Shtiya Builder digitizes this entire process, providing predictive analytics to identify municipal and financial distress before it hits public ledgers.  
The Interactive Map View is optimized for high-volume, macro-level spatial reconnaissance. The visual layers aggregate distress indicators across vast geographic areas, displaying 60-day mortgage latencies, municipal water shut-offs, and prolonged code enforcement violations regarding unpermitted construction or abandoned properties16. When the investor\_wholesaler clicks a parcel marker, the UI presents an automated contract generation interface, allowing the user to seamlessly transmit a digitally signed purchase option directly to the property owner.  
Cross-group integrations are continuous, dynamic, and highly transactional. The wholesaler interacts heavily with the Distressed Homeowner to negotiate off-market acquisitions and subsequently routes the secured, digitally signed contract to the Value-Add Developer for immediate assignment. Furthermore, the wholesaler relies on the Title & Escrow Officer to run preliminary algorithmic title searches to ensure the asset is unencumbered before committing any earnest resources.  
Financial mechanics rely entirely on $SHQL tokenized contract assignments. When the wholesaler secures a purchase option, it is minted as a non-fungible data object within the ecosystem. The Value-Add Developer executes a smart contract that transfers the $SHQL assignment fee to the wholesaler's wallet. The underlying smart contract instantly reassigns the equitable title rights to the new developer, logging the chain of custody immutably to prevent any off-chain assignment disputes or dual-contract fraud4.  
The investor\_value\_add persona, representing the Value-Add or Infill Developer, focuses on aggressive structural repositioning. This includes comprehensive gut-renovations, single-family to multi-family conversions, and the execution of SB 9 lot splits to double localized density8. The primary motivation is realizing a high Internal Rate of Return (IRR) through forced appreciation and accelerated construction timelines. The massive friction point removed by the platform is the operational chaos inherent in managing general contractors, permitting authorities, municipal inspectors, and hard money lenders simultaneously across multiple active job sites.  
The Interactive Map View for this persona renders complex architectural and environmental feasibility layers. The developer sees Standard Urban Storm Water Mitigation Plan (SUSMP) runoff requirements9, Alquist-Priolo Special Studies Zones indicating active fault lines such as the Whittier Fault9, and strict SB 9 eligibility filters verifying that a lot can be split equally into parcels no smaller than 1,200 square feet8. Clicking a parcel marker allows the developer to overlay preliminary Computer-Aided Design (CAD) footprints, calculate post-renovation Gross Rent Multipliers, and instantly query the local permit database for historical inspection failures or structural deficiencies.  
This sub-role operates as a central operational hub, integrating continuously with the General Contractor for physical execution, the Registered Architect for permit drawing generation, and the Institutional Hard-Money Lender for continuous construction debt facilities.  
The smart contract triggers are highly sophisticated, utilizing algorithmic construction draws to maintain liquidity. The investor\_value\_add deposits their equity tranche into a milestone-based smart contract, while the Institutional Lender deposits the corresponding debt portion11. As the General Contractor completes structural phases, the developer utilizes the platform to cryptographically sign off on the work. This N-of-M signature protocol, requiring approval from both the developer and the lender's designated inspector, automatically releases the proportional $SHQL funds directly to the contractor, entirely eliminating manual invoicing delays and preserving project momentum11.  
Designated as investor\_assembler, the Master Assembler or Ground-Up Developer represents an enterprise-tier user that orchestrates the acquisition of massive, contiguous tracts of urban land. Their objective is to demolish existing structures, petition for comprehensive rezoning, and erect high-density commercial or residential towers. The driving motivation is the deployment of massive capital stacks into generational assets, capitalizing on municipal rezoning initiatives and density bonuses. The primary pain point eliminated is the catastrophic holdout risk, representing the scenario where a single landowner halts a multi-million dollar assembly by refusing to sell or demanding exorbitant premiums late in the process.  
The Interactive Map View provides a macro-level, systemic view of the urban grid and its underlying regulatory mathematics. The layers display aggregated FAR upside, shadow studies, and theoretical massing envelopes. The map specifically highlights complete blocks where the Owner-Investor sub-roles have already achieved absolute consensus. Clicking on a macro-parcel marker opens a highly secure data room containing the cryptographically secured intent-to-sell agreements of the underlying owners, civil engineering soil reports detailing conditions in areas like the Puente Hills or local alluvial plains, and comprehensive environmental impact assessments10.  
The assembler integrates continuously with the Zoning Consultant to formally petition city councils for density bonuses and the Joint Venture Equity Partner to structure the complex Limited Partner (LP) and General Partner (GP) capital stacks. They also engage the Transactional Attorney to draft the overarching Purchase and Sale Agreements (PSAs) covering dozens of individual properties simultaneously.  
Financial triggers operate on a decentralized, trustless syndication model. The investor\_assembler deploys an institutional-grade smart contract that aggregates massive capital tranches from multiple Joint Venture participants. The contract holds the $SHQL capital in an immutable escrow until the Zoning Consultant successfully registers the lot consolidation with the municipality. Once an external oracle, such as the municipal property database, confirms the rezoning event, the smart contract deterministically executes the bulk acquisition, sweeping the $SHQL to the respective Owner-Investor wallets and transferring the master consolidated deed to the assembler's holding company, ensuring seamless execution of a massively complex transaction15.

## **Group 3: The Lender / Capital Provider**

The velocity of the Shtiya Builder ecosystem is entirely dependent on the frictionless, secure movement of capital. The RBAC model meticulously segments capital providers by their position in the capital stack, their specific risk profile, and the nature of the physical assets they underwrite, ensuring that financial data is only exposed to entities with verified underwriting intent.  
The lender\_institutional sub-role provides short-term, high-interest debt designed to fund rapid acquisitions and heavy construction phases. The motivation is securing high yields backed by tangible, first-position liens on the underlying real estate. The critical friction point resolved by the platform is the systemic mitigation of construction risk. Historically, lenders relied on slow, manual inspections to verify progress before releasing funds, causing massive downstream delays. The platform digitizes and dramatically accelerates this risk management protocol.  
The Interactive Map View for the institutional lender serves as a comprehensive risk-monitoring dashboard. The visual layers display Loan-to-Value (LTV) heatmaps across geographic regions, active default clusters, and real-time construction velocity metrics across their active portfolio. Clicking a specific parcel marker allows the lender to view a chronological, immutable feed of site photos, digital inspection reports, and budget variance analyses tied directly to the structural execution.  
This role integrates constantly with the Value-Add Developer and the General Contractor. The lender's API ingests the architectural Bill of Quantities (BoQ) generated by the Registered Architect to mathematically model the exact draw schedule and capital requirements before the loan is formally originated.  
Financial triggers are rooted in advanced computer vision and milestone release protocols. When a General Contractor submits photographic evidence of completed work, Gemini Vision algorithms process the imagery to verify structural milestones, such as framing completion or foundation pouring22. If the artificial intelligence confirms the milestone, and the Value-Add Developer cryptographically signs off, the lender\_institutional's programmatic criteria are satisfied. The smart contract immediately liquidates the designated $SHQL draw from the escrow vault and routes it to the contractor's wallet, ensuring that capital velocity perfectly matches physical construction velocity11.  
The lender\_heloc persona represents retail banking institutions or decentralized liquidity pools offering secondary debt products to existing homeowners seeking to extract equity. The motivation is the massive deployment of consumer credit backed by established residential equity. The pain point eliminated is the high customer acquisition cost and the prolonged, highly manual underwriting processes typical of traditional home equity lending environments.  
Within the Interactive Map View, the lender\_heloc observes aggregate equity metrics across neighborhoods. The layers display spatial concentrations of unleveraged equity and highly favorable credit profiles. When interacting with a parcel marker, the lender views the specific AVM variance, historical tax assessments, and the detailed proposed renovation scopes submitted by the Single Homeowner.  
Integrations are tightly bound to the Single Homeowner and the Title & Escrow Officer. The lender relies completely on the title officer to programmatically confirm that no superior liens or hidden encumbrances exist that would jeopardize their secondary lien position on the asset.  
Financial interactions are executed through automated underwriting APIs. When a Single Homeowner applies for a HELOC to fund a renovation, the system instantly compares the post-renovation AVM against the requested loan amount. If the parameters fall within the lender's JSON-defined risk schema, the system originates the loan automatically, funding the user's $SHQL wallet and simultaneously recording the secondary lien securely on the digital ledger7.  
Operating under the lender\_jv key, the Joint Venture Equity Partner represents private equity funds, family offices, or tokenized syndicates providing the massive risk capital necessary to fund block assemblies and ground-up development. The motivation is capturing the aggressive equity multiples generated by rezoning arbitrage and structural development. The major friction point resolved is the historical transparency of the capital stack; Limited Partners (LPs) historically suffered from opaque reporting, commingled funds, and severe information asymmetry.  
The Interactive Map View functions as a macroeconomic capital deployment interface. Layers illustrate municipal infrastructure investments, path-of-progress demographic shifts, and the real-time assembly progress of the Master Assembler. Clicking a parcel marker reveals the exact Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate) projections, the General Partner's historical performance metrics, and the current, real-time state of the equity syndication.  
The lender\_jv integrates primarily with the Master Assembler and the Transactional Attorney. The attorney ensures that the Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) operating agreement perfectly reflects the exact terms of the on-chain syndication logic.  
Financial triggers are dictated by decentralized autonomous organization (DAO) mechanics or strict multi-signature escrows. The lender\_jv deposits $SHQL directly into the assembly escrow. The smart contract guarantees that if the Master Assembler fails to secure the minimum contiguous acreage required by municipal code, or fails the environmental impact report regarding fault lines or hydrology, the $SHQL is automatically and algorithmically refunded to the equity partners. This cryptographically eliminates the GP's ability to misappropriate funds or trap LP capital in failed assemblies14.

## **Group 4: Legal Shield**

The Legal Shield group embeds municipal compliance, severe risk mitigation, and transaction finality directly into the platform's execution layer. By granting specialized legal professionals dedicated RBAC access, the system bridges the critical gap between immutable on-chain logic and the fluid, highly nuanced realities of off-chain jurisprudence and municipal bureaucracy.  
The legal\_transactional persona, representing the Transactional or Real Estate Attorney, drafts the master agreements, ensures deep regulatory compliance, and handles the intricate nuances of contract law that cannot be purely quantified in code. The motivation is massive billable efficiency and the drastic reduction of transactional liability. The pain point eliminated is the endless, error-prone reconciliation of differing contract versions scattered across fragmented email threads and physical document storage.  
The Interactive Map View for the attorney is primarily an informational overlay identifying title chain complexities, jurisdictional boundaries, and specific municipal overlays. Clicking a parcel marker opens the highly secure document repository, displaying the complete, immutable audit trail of every offer, counter-offer, and cryptographic digital signature associated with the property4.  
The attorney integrates universally across both the Investor and Lender groups. They are exclusively responsible for reviewing the off-chain written master agreement that governs the on-chain smart contract execution. As legal researchers consistently note, a smart contract is simply a self-executing software script; the paper or digital contract signed by the parties continues to absolutely control the legal relationship, necessitating attorney oversight to ensure the code reflects the legal intent4.  
Financial triggers for the attorney involve the authorization of terminal state transitions. Once the attorney verifies that all municipal transfer taxes are paid, compliance checks are complete, and the deed is properly executed, they utilize their cryptographic signature to trigger the final $SHQL settlement. This action moves funds from the buyer's escrow directly to the seller's wallet while simultaneously logging the transfer on the blockchain, finalizing the transaction.  
Operating via the legal\_title key, the Title & Escrow Officer clears historical encumbrances, issues binding title commitments, and acts as the ultimate neutral, third-party arbiter of the transaction. The motivation is the secure, error-free transfer of property rights. The platform resolves the immense pain point of delayed wire transfers, asynchronous lien releases, and escrow fraud.  
The Interactive Map View renders a continuous feed of municipal encumbrance data. Layers highlight properties with clouded titles, mechanic's liens filed by disgruntled contractors, or outstanding municipal fines that must be cleared prior to transfer. Clicking a marker allows the officer to upload the final title commitment directly to the secure, permissioned data room.  
The officer integrates seamlessly with every single party involved in a property transfer. They provide the critical oracle data to the smart contracts, confirming that the title is clean and ready for transfer.  
Financial triggers are entirely custodial in nature. The legal\_title officer strictly controls the ultimate release of the $SHQL earnest money. If a buyer breaches the contract terms, the officer executes a manual transaction to liquidate the escrow in favor of the seller. Because the funds are held in a programmatic vault rather than a traditional banking architecture, the settlement occurs in minutes rather than days, drastically increasing platform liquidity4.  
Assigned the legal\_expediter key, the Municipal Violation Expediter is a highly specialized agent that navigates the bureaucratic labyrinth of the Department of Building (DOB), Housing Preservation and Development (HPD), and Environmental Control Boards (ECB). The motivation is high-volume case resolution and the collection of fixed cure fees. The core pain point eliminated is the structural latency of municipal communication; the platform serves as a centralized, high-throughput dashboard for managing hundreds of concurrent hearings and violation cures.  
The Interactive Map View acts as a geographic triage board. The layers specifically render active Stop Work Orders (SWO) in bright red, alongside heatmaps of recent code enforcement sweeps in stringent jurisdictions, identifying issues such as unpermitted structural maintenance, commercial vehicles in residential zones, or illegal grading16. Clicking a parcel reveals the exact text of the municipal violation, time-stamped photographic evidence of the infraction, and the scheduled date for the administrative hearing.  
The expediter integrates heavily with the Distressed Homeowner and the General Contractor. If a contractor triggers an SWO by exceeding the scope of their municipal permit, the expediter is immediately pinged via the system to file a cure certificate and schedule an emergency hearing to lift the order.  
Financial triggers operate on a strict operational milestone basis. The distressed user or their capital backer locks the expediter's fee in a $SHQL escrow. Once the municipal database updates via an API call indicating the SWO has been successfully lifted or the violation cured, the smart contract reads this state change and automatically releases the $SHQL to the expediter's wallet, ensuring they are paid immediately upon successful resolution4.  
The legal\_loss\_mitigation persona acts as the critical intermediary between distressed homeowners and aggressive lending institutions, negotiating complex workout agreements, forbearance plans, and short sales. The motivation is preserving homeownership where mathematically possible or orchestrating a graceful, financially sound exit when necessary. The platform removes the friction of asymmetric information, ensuring the specialist has real-time, verified access to the homeowner's financial state.  
The Interactive Map View is strictly controlled via RBAC to ensure absolute compliance with financial privacy regulations. The specialist only sees parcels where the Distressed Homeowner has explicitly granted access. The layers display the ticking timeline toward the public auction date and the aggregate debt burden. Clicking the parcel provides the exact debt-to-income ratios, the current arrears balance, and the immutable communication logs with the servicer.  
Integrations involve the Distressed Homeowner and external servicing banks. If a short sale is deemed the only viable path to avoid foreclosure, the specialist integrates with the Wholesaler to solicit a cash offer that satisfies the bank's minimum net requirements for release of lien.  
Financial triggers are entirely centered on the workout resolution. The platform escrows the processing and negotiation fees. If a short sale is approved by the bank, the smart contract ensures the specialist receives their $SHQL fee directly from the closing proceeds prior to the final disbursement to the institutional lender, protecting the specialist's compensation.

## **Group 5: Contractor & Sub-Trade**

The Contractor group forms the literal physical execution layer of the architecture. The platform transforms the historically fragmented, paper-heavy, and litigation-prone construction management process into a deterministic, cryptographically verifiable supply chain driven by automated logic.  
Operating with the contractor\_gc key, the General Contractor oversees the entire project scope, manages disparate sub-trades, and acts as the primary point of accountability for physical progress on site. The driving motivation is maintaining continuous, uninterrupted cash flow and completely avoiding the systemic delays associated with traditional draw schedules. The platform solves the chronic industry pain point of 60-to-90-day payment latencies by linking payments directly to verified, immutable data4.  
The Interactive Map View allows the GC to monitor their active job sites across the region in real-time. Visual layers indicate critical logistical data: municipal material delivery zones, local traffic blockages, and proximity to specialized supply houses. Clicking an active site marker opens the comprehensive Project Management interface, detailing the exact Bill of Quantities, active municipal permits, and the critical SUSMP runoff requirements necessary for compliance in hillside developments or sensitive Alquist-Priolo zones9.  
The GC integrates centrally with the Value-Add Developer for overarching project direction, the Specialized Sub-Contractor for labor delegation, and the Institutional Lender for capital draw requests.  
The financial triggers represent the most advanced implementation of the platform's smart contract infrastructure. The GC operates within a highly specific payment-control layer where work packages are capped and funds are pre-locked in escrow before work begins11. Upon completing a specific milestone, such as rough framing, the GC submits a photo update. The system utilizes advanced computer vision models to verify the structural progress against the CAD models22. If validated, the GC requests the draw. The smart contract programmatically checks for active holds, verifies the N-of-M approvals from the developer and engineer, and instantaneously routes the $SHQL draw to the GC, dramatically compressing the payment cycle and virtually eliminating mechanics liens11.  
The contractor\_sub persona encompasses the highly specific trades—plumbing, electrical, masonry, and HVAC—responsible for executing the micro-tasks within the larger project scope. The motivation is securing a steady, reliable pipeline of localized work and ensuring absolutely guaranteed payment upon completion of their specific task. The primary pain point eliminated is the immense risk of the GC withholding payment due to upstream financing delays or fund commingling.  
The Interactive Map View shifts to a hyper-local, task-oriented perspective. The sub-contractor sees isolated maintenance tickets and specific work orders pinned to properties. Clicking a parcel marker reveals the exact technical specifications, the CAD schematics strictly relevant to their specific trade, and the pre-approved budget for the isolated task.  
The sub-contractor integrates primarily with the General Contractor and the Property Manager. A Property Manager can route a tenant's immediate plumbing ticket directly to the sub-contractor, bypassing the GC entirely for minor maintenance tasks to increase efficiency.  
Financial triggers utilize secondary smart contract splits. ## **Architectural Synthesis: The RBAC and JSON Infrastructure**

The efficacy of the Shtiya Builder platform relies absolutely on the strict enforcement of the permission boundaries detailed throughout this taxonomy. To accomplish this, the system utilizes an explicit JSON-based Role-Based Access Control matrix. This infrastructure dictates precisely what each of the twenty-two sub-roles can view, edit, export, and trigger, removing human error from access management2.  
In legacy systems, access control is often granted ad hoc, resulting in permission drift where users retain access to sensitive data long after their role changes1. Furthermore, legacy permissions are often "folder-shaped," meaning access is granted broadly rather than specifically30. The Shtiya Builder architecture solves this by ensuring permissions are scoped not just by role, but by specific property entity and geographic location. For example, a Property Manager can only view the financial data of the specific assets assigned to their scope, and external parties like a Transactional Attorney view data only until the transaction completes, at which point their access is programmatically revoked30.  
The separation of duties is programmatically enforced to ensure that the General Contractor who submits a draw request cannot mathematically be the same entity that approves the release30. The state machine evaluates the JSON policy at every single API endpoint. A simplified representation of the underlying routing logic resembles the following enterprise architecture7:

JSON  
"roles": \[  
  {  
    "roleName": "contractor\_gc",  
    "description": "Can submit draw requests and view site boundaries.",  
    "actions": \[  
      "Shtiya.Map/JobSite/read",  
      "Shtiya.SmartContract/Escrow/RequestDraw",  
      "Shtiya.Project/Tickets/write"  
    \]  
  },  
  {  
    "roleName": "lender\_institutional",  
    "description": "Can approve draws and view LTV risk data.",  
    "actions": \[  
      "Shtiya.Map/RiskData/read",  
      "Shtiya.SmartContract/Escrow/ApproveDraw",  
      "Shtiya.Project/Financials/export"  
    \]  
  }  
\]

This deterministic security model ensures that the Wholesaler cannot view the proprietary BoQ generated by the Registered Architect, and the Tenant cannot access the pre-foreclosure timelines managed by the Loss Mitigation specialist. Furthermore, every permission-sensitive action generates an immutable, traceable record in the audit log, capturing what was changed, by which role, and at what time1. By strictly scoping permissions to the precise intersection of the user's programmatic role and the specific property entity, the Shtiya Builder platform safely orchestrates the highly adversarial, high-stakes environment of urban real estate development into a frictionless, multi-sided marketplace30.
